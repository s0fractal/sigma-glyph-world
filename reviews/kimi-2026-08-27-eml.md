## Verdict

**H-EML-SHARE is not well-posed as a causal claim on a content-addressed store.** The CAS instrument conflates provenance by construction; it can measure *that* content collapses, but not *why*. N1 and N2 do not separate the hypothesized mechanism from the weaker "real != random" statement. A stronger null (N4, grammar-matched independent trees) is constructible and should be preregistered, but even N4 cannot fully disentangle construction-driven reuse from alphabet-driven coincidence on a CAS. The experiment should be narrowed to a descriptive census with no causal hypothesis, or the hypothesis should be reformulated as a claim about the *size spectrum* of shared subtrees — which is directly measurable and indirectly diagnostic.

EXP-002 is unaffected by this critique; its hypotheses are well-posed.

---

## (a) Do N1 and N2 separate construction-driven reuse from alphabet-driven coincidence?

**No.** They separate real trees from random trees, which is weaker and almost surely true.

**Why N1 fails the separation.** N1 draws random trees with the same size and leaf multiset as each real construction. For a tree of *n* internal nodes drawn from a 4-leaf alphabet, the expected number of coincident subtrees of size *k* is approximately binomial in the number of *k*-sized subtrees and the collision probability. With 692,375 total nodes, small subtrees (height 1-3) will collide by chance at a non-zero rate, but subtrees of size >=10 nodes will collide with probability negligible in the tree size. The real basis, by the transcription facts, contains subtrees of 504,554 nodes (`artanh`) that are embedded whole in later constructions. The ratio gap between real and N1 will be driven entirely by these large-subtree embeddings — but N1 has no large-subtree embeddings by construction, because it generates independent random trees. So N1 tests "do chained constructions share more than independent random noise?" The answer is yes by a margin of orders of magnitude, but that tells us nothing about whether the sharing is *because* of chaining or *because* the alphabet is small. A small alphabet does produce small-subtree collisions; N1 cannot measure whether those small collisions account for any appreciable fraction of the real sharing, because N1 lacks the large-subtree component entirely.

**Why N2 fails the separation.** N2 shuffles leaves on the real tree topology. This preserves the chaining-derived topology while destroying leaf-value-dependent content matches. If N2 shows less sharing than the real basis, it demonstrates that leaf-value identity contributes to sharing — which is true but orthogonal to the causal claim. The real basis shares large subtrees because later chains embed earlier ones *with their leaf structure intact*; N2 breaks that identity. N2 therefore measures "how much sharing depends on preserving the specific leaf values of chained primitives," not "how much sharing is caused by chaining rather than alphabet size."

**Both nulls share a common defect:** they compare the real basis against ensembles that differ in *every* structural property simultaneously (size distribution, depth distribution, branching correlation, chaining topology, and leaf configuration). A difference in sharing cannot be attributed to any single property. The hypothesis asks whether sharing is driven by *one specific* property (construction-driven reuse); the nulls vary too many properties at once to isolate it.

## (b) A constructible null that does the work

**N4 — Grammar-matched independent-tree null.** This null preserves the EML grammar, the alphabet, and the per-construction size distribution, but eliminates the chaining structure. It is a strict refinement of N1.

**Draw procedure (preregisterable):**

For each construction *f* in the basis:
1. Let *n*(*f*) = number of internal `eml` nodes in *f*'s pure-EML expansion (from `basis.json`, precomputed).
2. Let *L*(*f*) = multiset of leaf symbols in *f*'s pure-EML expansion.
3. Generate a random binary tree with exactly *n*(*f*) internal nodes and leaf multiset *L*(*f*), uniformly from the set of such trees. Internal nodes are labelled `eml`; leaves are labelled according to *L*(*f*).
4. The random tree is generated by a recursive procedure: choose a split of the internal nodes between left and right subtrees uniformly from the valid splits, then recurse; choose a split of the leaf multiset uniformly from the valid splits, then recurse. Base case: 0 internal nodes -> a single leaf drawn from *L*(*f*) without replacement.
5. Draw 100 trees per construction, seeded by `sha256("EML-EXP-001/N4/{f}/{draw_index}")[:16]` interpreted as a big-endian integer, using the same draw-index convention as N1/N2.

**Measured quantities:** `size_tree`, `size_dag`, `ratio` per construction and for the union *U* of all 32 trees in each draw. Report mean and minimum across the 100 draws; the real basis passes a level only against the minimum.

**Why N4 is stronger than N1.** N1 does not fix the grammar. A "size-matched random tree" could have arbitrary arity, arbitrary node labels, or a different distribution of tree shapes. N4 fixes the grammar to exactly `{eml/2, 1, x, y, E}` and fixes the leaf multiset per construction. The only remaining degree of freedom is the tree shape and the leaf ordering. If the real basis beats N4, the excess sharing is attributable to something beyond independent random generation from the same grammar and size — which includes chaining, but also any other non-random structure in the real basis.

**N5 — Chain-topology-preserving content-permutation null (optional, more aggressive).** This null preserves the chaining syntactic form but breaks the specific content identity of reused primitives.

**Draw procedure (preregisterable):**

For each construction *f* (ordered 1..32 as in the paper):
1. Identify the set *D*(*f*) of primitive names referenced in *f*'s `chain_sexpr` (from `basis.json`).
2. For each *d* in *D*(*f*), let *s*(*d*) = number of `eml` nodes in *d*'s pure-EML expansion.
3. Construct a permutation pool *P*(*f*) = {constructions *g* < *f* : |*s*(*g*) - *s*(*d*)| / *s*(*d*) <= 0.1 and hash(*g*) != hash(*d*)}. If *P*(*f*) is empty, relax the size constraint to the closest available *g* with hash(*g*) != hash(*d*).
4. Replace each occurrence of *d* in *f*'s `chain_sexpr` with a uniform draw from *P*(*f*).
5. Expand the modified chain to pure EML via the same mechanical substitution as `build_basis.py`.
6. Draw 100 such permuted chains per construction, seeded by `sha256("EML-EXP-001/N5/{f}/{draw_index}")[:16]`.

**What N5 tests.** If sharing in N5 is comparable to the real basis, then the chaining *process* (building each construction from earlier ones) is sufficient to produce the observed sharing, regardless of which specific primitives are chained. If sharing drops significantly, then the *specific content* of the chained primitives — i.e., the actual construction-driven reuse of particular subtrees — is the dominant driver.

**Caveat on N5.** Because replacements are drawn from earlier constructions, some content identity may survive by chance (e.g., replacing `exp` with `e` if both happen to have the same hash — though they do not in this basis). The size-matching constraint reduces but does not eliminate this. N5 is therefore a probe of the *specificity* of reuse, not a perfect elimination of it.

## (c) Is the distinction meaningless under CAS identity? What should the experiment measure instead?

**The causal distinction is not directly testable on a CAS, but it is not meaningless.** The CAS instrument is provenance-agnostic by design: it reports one hash for two identical subtrees whether they are identical because one was derived from the other or because they were generated independently. Therefore, no null model — N1, N2, N4, or N5 — can definitively attribute a shared address to "construction-driven reuse" rather than "alphabet-driven coincidence." The CAS cannot see the "because."

**However, the distinction is indirectly diagnostic via the size spectrum.** Alphabet-driven coincidence in a 4-leaf grammar produces shared subtrees that are small (height 1-3, perhaps a few dozen nodes at most). Construction-driven reuse in a chained basis produces shared subtrees that are large (thousands to hundreds of thousands of nodes, corresponding to entire primitive embeddings). The CAS cannot tell us *why* a subtree is shared, but it *can* tell us *how large* the shared subtrees are. A basis where 90% of the DAG savings come from subtrees of >=1,000 nodes is overwhelmingly dominated by construction-driven reuse, because independent random generation from a 4-leaf alphabet cannot produce such large coincident subtrees at any appreciable rate.

**Recommended redesign:**

1. **Narrow H-EML-SHARE to a descriptive claim.** Replace the causal wording with:
   > **H-EML-SHARE-revised:** The Odrzywolek basis exhibits sharing that exceeds independent random generation from the same EML grammar (N4), and the excess is concentrated in subtrees larger than any plausible alphabet-coincidence threshold.

2. **Add a size-spectrum measurement.** For each distinct hash in the union store, record its subtree size (node count). Report:
   - The cumulative distribution of shared-subtree sizes.
   - The fraction of `size_tree(U) - size_dag(U)` savings attributable to subtrees above a preregistered size threshold (e.g., >=100 nodes, >=1,000 nodes).
   - The largest shared subtree size and the construction pair(s) that share it.

3. **Keep N1 and N2** as weak descriptive baselines, but do not interpret them as testing the causal hypothesis. Interpret them as: "the basis shares more than random trees / leaf-shuffled trees," full stop.

4. **Add N4** as the primary null. N4 is constructible, preregisterable, and strictly stronger than N1.

5. **Retain `cross_only(U)` and per-function statistics** as descriptive measurements. These are meaningful and falsifiable: if `cross_only(U)` is near zero, then sharing is almost entirely within-function, which would be surprising for a chained basis.

6. **Drop the "not merely because a two-symbol alphabet" clause** from the hypothesis. It cannot be adjudicated by the instrument. Instead, report the size spectrum as a `FACT` line and let the reader draw the inference.

**What would make the redesigned experiment worthless:** Headlining the size spectrum as "proof" of construction-driven reuse (it is not proof, it is diagnostic); using a size threshold chosen post-hoc; dropping N4 after seeing the data.

### EML-EXP-001

**A1.** `ratio(U) < 0.001` — the union store is at least three orders of magnitude below the tree count. Falsifier: `ratio(U) >= 0.001`.

**A2.** `cross_only(U) >= 0.65 * size_dag(U)` — more than two-thirds of distinct stored nodes appear in at least two constructions, because the chain expansion embeds early primitives (`e`, `exp`, `ln`, `neg1`, `two`, etc.) in nearly every later construction. Falsifier: `cross_only(U) < 0.65 * size_dag(U)`.

**A3.** At least **25** of 32 constructions have `ratio(f) < min(N1)` per-function. The constructions that do not beat N1's minimum are exactly the smallest ones (`e`, `exp`, `ln`, `neg1`, `two`, `minus`, `sigma`) where N1 also produces trees with `ratio = 1.0` (no sharing possible at that size). All constructions with `nodes(f) > 10` beat N1 by a wide margin. Falsifier: fewer than 25 beat N1's minimum.

**A4.** The largest shared subtree in the union store has **> 100,000 nodes** and is shared by at least **3** distinct constructions. Falsifier: largest shared subtree <= 100,000 nodes or shared by < 3 constructions.

### EML-EXP-002

**A5.** Exactly **6** of the 22 included constructions have `n* > 20` or `NONE`. This is between P-draft's "at least one" and P-fable's "at least half"; the deep chain constructions accumulate error, but fewer than half are irrecoverable at n <= 20. Falsifier: fewer than 6 or more than 6.

**A6.** `e`, `exp`, `ln` reach 1e-3 at `n <= 8` under round-to-nearest — shallower than P-fable's <= 16. These are the three shallowest purely-real constructions (1-7 nodes each) and their error propagation path is short. Falsifier: any of the three requires `n > 8`.

**A7.** Spearman rho(`depth(f)`, `n*(f)`) >= **0.55** over constructions with finite `n*`, excluding `e`, `exp`, `ln` as floor outliers. The correlation is stronger than P-draft's >= 0.5 because depth is the dominant error-accumulation mechanism in chained `eml` evaluations. Falsifier: rho < 0.55.

---

**Summary position:** The experiment should run, but H-EML-SHARE should be reformulated before the harness executes. The descriptive measurements (`ratio(U)`, `cross_only(U)`, size spectrum, per-function null exceedance) are valuable and should be retained. N4 should be added to the preregistration. The causal claim should be demoted to a `DERIVATION` from the size-spectrum data, not a hypothesis scored by the harness.
