## Findings

### 1. BLOCKER — the “explicit tree, no sharing” λ machine actually shares objects

The preregistration pins an “explicit syntax tree” with “no sharing”
([KAPPA-EXP-001 preregistration](../experiments/KAPPA-EXP-001-preregistration.md#the-machine-pinned)).
The implementation does not satisfy that representation contract.

[`substitute`](../experiments/kappa-exp-001/lambda_machine.py#L75-L92) returns the
same `value` object for every substituted variable occurrence. In the minimal
duplication `(λx. p x x) y`, both result positions therefore point to the same
Python object. `Term.size` still adds both occurrence weights, so it reports the
size of a fully expanded abstract tree while the executable object graph is a
shared DAG.

Minimal reproducer:

```python
from lambda_machine import family, normalize

nf = normalize(family(1), "S_in", 100)["normal_form"]
assert nf.fun.arg is nf.arg
```

Observed reachable-object counts:

| strategy | n | reported `normal_form.size` | distinct Python term objects |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `S_in` | 4 | 61 | 10 |
| `S_in` | 8 | 1021 | 18 |
| `S_out` | 4 | 61 | 32 |
| `S_out` | 8 | 1021 | 512 |

This changes the scientific meaning of `peak`. Under the implementation's
actual representation, `S_in` does **not** materialize an exponential state; its
normal form is a linear-size DAG. The experiment measures an
occurrence-weighted logical tree size and calls it materialized state. Likewise,
`C_size` claims to charge for materialized copies that the harness does not
allocate.

The KAPPA-EXP-002 cross-check makes the mismatch sharper. At `n=8`, the Book I
oracle's normal form has 1021 distinct Python objects, while KAPPA-EXP-001
`S_in` has 18, even though both report `size_tree = 1021`. Equality of those two
numbers is not equality of representation or physical materialization.

**Impact:** KAPPA-EXP-001's central resource interpretation is invalid as
implemented. KAPPA-EXP-003 and KAPPA-EXP-005 inherit the same machine, so their
language about copies, recomputation, and materialized space is also not an
empirical statement about the executing representation. The algebra over the
occurrence-weighted metric may remain correct, but that is a different claim.

**Proposal:** preregister KAPPA-EXP-006 as a representation audit. Either:

1. implement genuine no-sharing substitution with fresh nodes and measure
   cumulative allocations plus peak reachable/live nodes; or
2. retain aliasing, rename the model honestly as a persistent DAG with
   occurrence-weighted tree size, and stop equating `size` with actual
   materialization.

In both versions record, independently, logical occurrence size, distinct
reachable objects, distinct content hashes, cumulative allocations, peak live
objects, reduction steps, and charged cost. Add an aliasing control that fails
the no-sharing machine if duplicated positions share object identity. This is a
representation change, so it needs a fresh experiment rather than a patch to the
frozen result.

### 2. BLOCKER — KAPPA-EXP-005 reports an asymptotic refutation it has not proved

The preregistration correctly says a finite grid cannot certify boundedness and
that observed growth is not a proof of a closed form
([reporting rule](../experiments/KAPPA-EXP-005-preregistration.md#reporting-rule-unchanged)).
The result then declares `H-SPREAD-DUP` refuted and
`spread → k + 9/4`, while its own limitations section says the needed
closed-form proof of peak over the whole family is `UNKNOWN`
([RESULT](../experiments/kappa-exp-005/RESULT.md#what-this-does-not-establish)).

The validator proves two cost formulae on the 40 frozen rows. It does **not**
prove the peak formula. Instead it checks that peak excess is equal on the last
three measured `n` values for each `k`
([`validate.py`](../experiments/kappa-exp-005/validate.py#L77-L87)) and then prints
`PASS: H-SPREAD-DUP refuted`.

Three equal tail observations do not establish an all-`n`, all-`k` limit. Exact
cost recurrences are insufficient because `spread` also contains both
strategies' peaks. The experiment decisively refutes its preregistered numerical
prediction; it does not yet refute finiteness of `sup_t spread(t)`.

**Proposal:** until a proof exists, change the epistemic status to:

```text
FACT: the preregistered prediction failed on the frozen grid.
HYPOTHESIS: the observed family has limit k + 9/4.
UNKNOWN: whether H-SPREAD-DUP is false.
```

Then derive an exact peak recurrence or a uniform upper bound for `S_out` and a
matching lower bound for `S_in`, for all `n,k`. Encode that theorem separately
from the measurement check and add a mutation that preserves the last three
points but violates an earlier recurrence. Only the theorem, not another wider
grid, can close the asymptotic claim.

### 3. MAJOR — “a single-integer budget must pin strategy” does not follow

The result generalizes from two selected cost models to “no choice of
materialization-charging cost model removes” the mechanism, then calls strategy
pinning a derived requirement
([KAPPA-EXP-005 result](../experiments/kappa-exp-005/RESULT.md#consequence-for-track-a)).

The experiments show that charged cost can differ greatly by strategy under the
chosen models. That is a statement about **tightness, efficiency, or
comparability**. It is not a theorem that a hard one-integer safety budget
requires a pinned strategy. The repository's own materialization-charging
inequality gives a safe state bound strategy-by-strategy; a conservative
strategy-agnostic price or worst-case reservation can remain safe, although it
may waste capacity. Unbounded spread does not distinguish those properties.

The phrase “no choice ... removes it” is also universally quantified over cost
models, while only `C_size` and `C_dup` were studied.

**Proposal:** split the desired property into at least:

- hard-cap safety;
- constant-competitive/tight prepayment;
- work conservation or utilization;
- strategy-independent comparability.

Then state the quantifiers. A defensible successor claim may be that no
strategy-independent **constant-competitive** charge exists for this family
under a specified class of local cost models. That requires defining the class
and proving a lower bound. The current data do not make strategy pinning a
requirement for safety.

### 4. MAJOR — the target quantity slips between a global machine κ and a family κ

KAPPA-EXP-001 defines `κ_M(t)` and then defines “`κ_M` over the family” as
`sup_n κ_M(h_n)`
([definition](../experiments/KAPPA-EXP-001-preregistration.md#definition-of-κ)).
The headline conclusion, however, is that κ is not an invariant of the calculus.

Those are not automatically the same statement. If machine-level κ means the
supremum over all normalizing runs, one family can show an unbounded pointwise
strategy ratio without showing that the two global suprema differ. Both global
suprema might be infinite because of other terms. Conversely, if κ is indexed by
a workload family, it is not “the κ of a calculus” in the first place.

**Proposal:** use an explicit signature such as
`κ(M, T) = sup_{t in T} (peak_M(t)-1)/cost_M(t)` and never erase `T`. Decide
whether the theorem under attack is:

1. equality of global suprema;
2. a uniform pointwise comparison across strategies on the same term; or
3. an ordering of machines on a declared workload distribution.

The current `h_n` result supports a pointwise non-uniformity claim under the
abstract occurrence metric. It does not by itself identify a global calculus
constant.

### 5. MAJOR — P003 screening decisions are not auditable from committed evidence

The 168 search checkpoints are committed, which is good. The evidence used for
the 60 substantive screening decisions is not. The only P003 ignore rule is
[`evidence-cache/`](../experiments/scope-pilot-003/.gitignore), and the repository
tracks zero files from that directory.

[`record_screening.py`](../experiments/scope-pilot-003/record_screening.py#L36-L63)
copies hashes of the issue and comments API responses into the log. The raw or
minimized evidence corresponding to those hashes is absent. The sole admitted
incident additionally relies on two fix commits, but the screening schema has no
digest or frozen evidence object for either commit. `screen.py --check` validates
identity, prefix order, enums, and caps; it does not and cannot verify the
researcher's evidential reasons.

**Impact:** another reviewer can reproduce that 60 decisions were written in
order, but cannot reproduce or audit why they were correct at the retrieved
time. The strongest `FACT` in the screening result — the admitted incident's
merged test and release-note contract — depends on live URLs that are neither
content-addressed nor covered by the committed response hashes.

**Proposal:** commit minimized immutable evidence manifests for every screened
candidate, with a digest for every cited issue, comment page, commit, changed
blob, regression test, documentation line, and adjudication. Preserve researcher
judgment as judgment; do not imply `screen.py` validates its substance. At
minimum, make the admitted and `CONTRACT_DISPUTED` cases independently auditable
before using them to design the next frame.

### 6. MAJOR — “frame failure, not instrument failure” is not identified, and the proposed repair changes the estimand

The P003 result attributes the outcome to the sampling frame rather than the
instrument. But the admission gate is part of the instrument, and eight
candidates were rejected specifically because codebook v1's contract tier was
not met. The result itself says the binding scarcity is “adjudicated contracts.”
That is an interaction between the frame and the instrument, not evidence that
one side alone caused the failure.

There is no comparison frame, relaxed admission arm, or independent
adjudication, so the causal attribution “frame, not instrument” is unsupported.
The honest status is `CONTESTED`: the query terms have poor directional
precision, and the codebook demands evidence rarely present in issue threads.

The proposed successor — sample from merged regressions, changelogs, and fixes —
creates a second problem. It conditions inclusion on resolution, maintenance,
and public documentation. That may be excellent for calibrating the codebook,
but it estimates mechanisms among **documented fixed incidents**, not prevalence
among verification-infrastructure incidents. It cannot answer `H-SCOPE`'s
incident-population comparison without a selection model.

**Proposal:** name the estimand before P004. Use a resolution-derived corpus only
as an instrument-calibration or evidence-augmentation corpus. For prevalence,
retain an incident-based probability sample and retrieve resolution evidence
after selection; record unresolved/missing contracts as outcomes rather than
excluding them. If the public record cannot support that estimand, retire the
prevalence question explicitly instead of answering a resolution-conditioned
replacement.

### 7. MAJOR — the root green suite permits vacuous success and silent dependency skips

The `screen.py` module says green cannot silently mean “screening skipped,” but
when `screening-log.json` is absent it prints `PASS` and exits 0
([`screen.py`](../experiments/scope-pilot-003/screen.py#L153-L165)). KAPPA
measurement and validation scripts similarly print `PASS: ... not measured yet`
when their frozen measurements are absent. KAPPA-EXP-002 exits 0 with `SKIPPED`
when the external pinned oracle is absent or has a different digest
([`oracle.py`](../experiments/kappa-exp-002/oracle.py#L238-L250)).

Consequently, `tools/test-all.sh` is a progress reporter, not a gate for the
published repository state. A commit deleting a result, a screening log, or the
external oracle can remain green. `git diff --check` does not detect missing
tracked artifacts in the committed tree.

**Proposal:** separate two commands:

- `test-progress` may permit `NOT_STARTED` and `SKIPPED`;
- `test-release` must enforce an exact manifest of required artifacts and
  terminal states for the current README claims, and treat skip as non-zero.

Add negative mutation tests that delete each required artifact and corrupt each
frozen digest. The gate should fail on the deletion, not merely on malformed
content. Print a closed set of executed, skipped, failed, and absent controls.

### 8. MODERATE — evidence comment retrieval silently truncates at 100

[`fetch_evidence.py`](../experiments/scope-pilot-003/fetch_evidence.py#L34-L80)
requests `per_page=100` but does not paginate. It records `comment_count` as
`len(comments)`, not the issue's declared total or a completeness flag.

In the current local evidence cache, positions 44 (`astral-sh/ruff#1904`) and 59
(`rocq-prover/rocq#12487`) each contain exactly 100 fetched comments. The harness
cannot tell whether later comments were omitted, and those later comments could
contain maintainer adjudication, a fixing commit, or a contradiction that changes
the screening reason.

This probably cannot rescue P003's minimum of eight by itself, but it weakens the
claim that all 60 decisions used complete primary threads.

**Proposal:** follow pagination links to exhaustion, bind every page digest and
the provider-reported total, and reject screening when evidence completeness is
unknown. Record explicit `comments_complete` rather than overloading a fetched
length as a total count.

### 9. MODERATE — repository sampling stratum is forced to become incident control kind

For an admitted entry, `screen.py` requires
`sampling_assessment.control_kind == frozen["stratum"]`
([`screen.py`](../experiments/scope-pilot-003/screen.py#L76-L86)). This turns a
repository-level frame label into an incident-level observation by construction.
The later minimum of three “control kinds” can therefore be satisfied by sampling
metadata even if an incident actually belongs to another kind.

**Proposal:** keep `sampling_stratum` and `observed_control_kind` separate. Permit
misclassification as data. Apply diversity requirements either to the frozen
sampling strata (and call them strata) or to independently adjudicated incident
kinds, never to a field forced equal to the frame.

### 10. MINOR — generated CPython bytecode is tracked

The repository tracks three `*.cpython-314.pyc` files under `__pycache__` and has
no root ignore rule for Python bytecode. They are interpreter-specific generated
artifacts, add noise, and are not part of any declared research receipt.

**Proposal:** remove them in a separate hygiene commit and ignore
`__pycache__/`/`*.pyc`. If executable-environment provenance matters, pin and
report the Python version explicitly instead of committing bytecode.

## Recommended order of work

1. Append an erratum that downgrades KAPPA-EXP-005's asymptotic refutation and
   Track A's “derived requirement.”
2. Preregister KAPPA-EXP-006 around representation identity and real allocation;
   do not reuse the current κ numbers as measurements of physical materialization.
3. Add a release-state manifest and deletion mutation tests so the published
   claims cannot pass with missing artifacts or a skipped oracle.
4. Freeze auditable P003 evidence manifests and re-audit the 60 screening
   decisions before using their reason distribution as a design premise.
5. Define the Track C estimand before choosing a resolution-based successor
   frame. Use resolution sampling for calibration, not silently for prevalence.

The most valuable next question is not another larger grid. It is whether the
claimed phenomenon survives when “tree,” “sharing,” “materialized state,” and
“actual work” are four separately measured quantities rather than four names for
one cached `size` field.
